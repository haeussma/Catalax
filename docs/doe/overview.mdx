Before running an experiment it is worth asking how much it will teach you. `catalax.doe` answers that question for any Catalax model: given a candidate design (which initial conditions to prepare and when to sample), it computes how much the measurements are expected to shrink the uncertainty of each kinetic parameter. Comparing candidate lab protocols, or checking that every parameter is identifiable before spending time at the bench, needs nothing more than the model and its priors.

## What the module computes

### Fisher information in log parameters

A design is a set of experimental arms. Each arm fixes the initial conditions of the states and constants, and a list of sampling times. For the free parameters $\theta$ (all parameters with `constant=False`), `catalax.doe` computes the sensitivities of every observable state at every sampling time with respect to $\log \theta$. Working in log space makes the result dimensionless: a design's score does not change when $K_m$ is reported in mM instead of µM.

The sensitivities are whitened by the observation noise, $w = \frac{1}{\mathrm{sd}(y)} \frac{\partial y}{\partial \log \theta}$, and the Fisher information sums over arms, times and observable states:

$$
F(\theta) = \sum_{\text{arms}} \sum_{t} \sum_{\text{observables}} w\, w^\top
$$

Two noise models are provided. `Homoskedastic(sigma)` has a constant standard deviation. `Proportional(cv, floor)` has $\mathrm{sd}(y) = \sqrt{\text{floor}^2 + (\text{cv} \cdot y)^2}$, the usual model for HPLC-type assays whose error grows with the measured amount. Any object with `scale(prediction)` and `whiten(sensitivities, prediction)` methods works as a noise model.

### Posterior shrinkage and efficiency

Because $F$ depends on the unknown $\theta$, `evaluate_design` averages over draws $\theta_i$ from the prior. For each draw, the data are summarised by a Gaussian likelihood of $\log\theta$ with precision $F(\theta_i)$, centred where a noisy dataset would put it. That likelihood is combined with the **exact** prior by importance sampling: 65,536 samples from a proposal centred on the draw's posterior, weighted by prior × likelihood over the proposal's density, and the posterior sd of each $\log\theta_j$ is the weighted sd of those samples. Averaged over the draws, this is the expected posterior sd after the experiment. It is reported as `relative_sd`: an sd of 0.21 in $\log\theta$ reads as about ±21% at one sd, or ×/÷ $e^{0.21} = 1.23$.

The **efficiency** of parameter $j$ is the fraction of its prior uncertainty the design removes:

$$
e_j = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{sd}_{\text{post},j}}{\mathrm{sd}_{\text{prior},j}}
$$

An efficiency of 0 means the design teaches nothing beyond the prior, 1 means the parameter is pinned exactly. A prior of $k_\text{cat}$ = 5–20 has a log sd of 0.38 (±38%); a design that leaves ±21% has an efficiency of $1 - 0.21/0.38 = 0.44$. A design that teaches almost nothing can come out slightly below 0, because some possible datasets push the posterior against a bound of the prior. `evaluate_design` reports the mean efficiency per parameter and the **maximin** efficiency, $\mathbb{E}_\theta[\min_j e_j]$, the expected efficiency of the worst-determined parameter. A good design leaves no parameter close to its prior, so maximin is the number to compare designs by.

**The search uses a cheaper score.** `optimize_design` needs a smooth score it can differentiate thousands of times, so it uses the Laplace approximation of the posterior,

$$
\Sigma_\text{post} = \left(F(\theta) + \Sigma_0^{-1}\right)^{-1},
$$

with $\Sigma_0$ the prior covariance of $\log\theta$. A `Uniform` prior then becomes a Gaussian of the same log variance, which has curvature the box does not have and ignores the box's edges. The Laplace numbers can be off by a factor of 1.5 either way, but Laplace ranks designs correctly. A search on the reweighted score found the same design (see [below](#how-far-to-trust-the-numbers)). So the search is Laplace and the report is reweighted.

## Usage

A design is an ordinary `Dataset` holding one initial-condition-only measurement per arm. The `time` argument of `add_initial` sets the sampling times.

```python
import jax
import catalax as ctx
import catalax.doe as cdoe
import catalax.mcmc as cmc

ctx.enable_x64()  # Fisher matrices are often too ill-conditioned for float32

model = ctx.Model(name="Michaelis-Menten")
model.add_state("s, p, e")
model.add_ode("s", "-kcat * e * s / (k_m + s)", observable=True)
model.add_ode("p", "kcat * e * s / (k_m + s)", observable=False)
model.add_ode("e", "0", observable=False)

model.parameters["kcat"].value = 10.0
model.parameters["kcat"].prior = cmc.priors.Uniform(low=5.0, high=20.0)
model.parameters["k_m"].value = 100.0
model.parameters["k_m"].prior = cmc.priors.Uniform(low=50.0, high=200.0)

times = [10.0, 40.0, 80.0, 150.0, 300.0]

low_only = ctx.Dataset.from_model(model)
low_only.add_initial(time=times, s=50.0, p=0.0, e=0.1)
low_only.add_initial(time=times, s=60.0, p=0.0, e=0.1)

spread = ctx.Dataset.from_model(model)
spread.add_initial(time=times, s=50.0, p=0.0, e=0.1)
spread.add_initial(time=times, s=400.0, p=0.0, e=0.1)

noise = cdoe.Proportional(cv=0.05, floor=0.5)
key = jax.random.PRNGKey(0)

for name, design in [("low only", low_only), ("spread", spread)]:
    report = cdoe.evaluate_design(model, design, noise, key=key)
    print(name)
    print(report)
```

```text
low only
k_m   ±23% (×/÷1.25)  prior ±38%  efficiency 0.41
kcat  ±18% (×/÷1.20)  prior ±38%  efficiency 0.53
maximin 0.41 over 256/256 draws; ESS min 9216, median 36412
spread
k_m   ±9% (×/÷1.09)  prior ±38%  efficiency 0.77
kcat  ±6% (×/÷1.06)  prior ±38%  efficiency 0.85
maximin 0.77 over 256/256 draws; ESS min 5176, median 51173
```

Each line gives the expected posterior uncertainty of a parameter after the experiment, its prior uncertainty and the efficiency. The numbers are also fields of the report: `report.relative_sd`, `report.prior_relative_sd`, `report.efficiency` and `report.maximin`. Use the same `key` for every candidate so they are scored on the same prior draws.

To look at the information at a single parameter vector, the model's current values, use `fisher_information`:

```python
info = cdoe.fisher_information(model, spread, noise)
info.matrix           # (n_free, n_free), rows and columns in info.parameter_order
info.parameter_order  # ['k_m', 'kcat'], alphabetical like get_parameter_order()
info.valid            # False if any simulation failed to solve
```

Parameters marked `constant=True` are left out of the matrix and enter the simulation at their `value`. Only states whose ODE is marked `observable=True` contribute measurements. Declare `observable` explicitly for every state.

### States and constants

Every initial condition in a design is a design variable, whether it belongs to a state or to a constant. `catalax.doe` makes no distinction between enzymes, substrates, products or any other species. A state is measured if and only if its ODE is `observable=True`, and it changes only as its rate says: `"0"` keeps it at its initial amount, and a term such as `"-k_inact * e"` adds a parameter the design informs like any other. A constant cannot change during the reaction. The two are interchangeable for a species that does not change: for the bi-substrate model, the Fisher matrices with the enzyme as a constant and as an unobservable zero-rate state agree to 1.2e-8.

## Optimising a design

`optimize_design` searches the initial conditions of `n_arms` experiments for the design with the highest expected maximin efficiency, and returns it as a `Dataset` ready for the lab. Continuing the Michaelis–Menten example:

```python
spec = {
    "s": (10.0, 1000.0),  # free: each arm picks its substrate inside the box
    "p": (0.0, 0.0),      # fixed: no product at the start
    "e": (0.1, 0.1),      # fixed: one enzyme concentration
}

result = cdoe.optimize_design(
    model,
    spec,
    noise,
    n_arms=2,
    times=times,
    key=jax.random.PRNGKey(1),
    n_draws=16,
)

for measurement in result.dataset.measurements:
    print(measurement.initial_conditions)
print(result.report)
print(result.restart_scores)
```

```text
{'s': 39.83616504613273, 'p': 0.0, 'e': 0.1}
{'s': 434.20603317816995, 'p': 0.0, 'e': 0.1}
k_m   ±9% (×/÷1.10)  prior ±38%  efficiency 0.76
kcat  ±6% (×/÷1.06)  prior ±38%  efficiency 0.84
maximin 0.76 over 256/256 draws; ESS min 6000, median 51805
[0.7299377146478052, 0.7299377189625851, 0.729937724351732, 0.7299377210832458, 0.72993772109689, 0.7299377145330183, 0.7299377185526345, 0.7299377156301939]
```

The **spec** maps every state and constant of the model to a range `(low, high)`. There are no defaults: an initial condition the spec leaves out is an error, whether it belongs to an enzyme, a substrate or a product. `low == high` fixes that initial condition at `low` in every arm; `low < high` lets every arm choose its own value inside the box. The **sampling times** are fixed: every arm is sampled at `times`. Optimising them tends to specialise the design toward the prior median and to give up the tails of the prior.

The search runs `n_restarts` gradient ascents (Adam) from Latin-hypercube starts. Each ascent maximises a smoothed maximin over `n_draws` fixed prior draws, and the restarts are then ranked on the exact maximin over `n_rank_draws` separate draws. `result.report` scores the winner on a third set of draws, because the winner's score on the draws it was selected on is biased upwards. Restarts that fail to solve on any ranking draw are dropped, since averaging over only the draws that solved would reward a design for failing; if every restart fails, `optimize_design` raises and suggests narrowing the bounds, raising `max_steps` or using a stiff solver.

**`restart_scores`** is the maximin of every restart on the ranking draws, under the Laplace score the search uses, so it differs from `result.report.maximin`. The report also uses other draws than the `spread` example above. Scored on one `key`, the two designs are within 0.001 of each other, `spread` slightly ahead under the report: the hand-picked design was already close to the optimum, and at that distance the search's Laplace score and the reweighted report can disagree. To compare designs, score them all with `evaluate_design` on one `key`. When they agree, the landscape has one basin and the restarts were insurance. When some fall well short, the landscape has several basins and the restarts were necessary. In that case raise `n_restarts` and check that the best score holds up with a different `key`. Use at least a few restarts; a single ascent can settle in a poor basin.

**Cost** is linear in the number of prior draws and in the number of restarts. One gradient step for a bi-substrate model (4 states, 4 arms × 9 times, CPU, float64), and the whole search on an M4 Pro (14 cores):

| `n_draws` | per step | 8 restarts × 300 steps | 24 restarts × 600 steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 27 ms | 13 s | 41 s |
| 64 | 105 ms | 22 s | 70 s |
| 256 | 499 ms | 80 s | 4.6 min |

Explore with 16 draws, and use more for the design you run in the lab.

**Cores.** The restarts run in parallel threads, `n_workers` of them, by default one per core up to `n_restarts`; the result does not depend on `n_workers`. The default counts the cores the process may use, so a SLURM job or `taskset` is honoured. A Docker `--cpus` quota is not: set `n_workers` and `ctx.set_host_count(n)` yourself there. On an Apple chip the efficiency cores count too but are slower, so set `n_workers` to the number of performance cores when `n_restarts` exceeds it.

## Closing the loop

After round 1 has been measured, fit it with `run_mcmc` and design round 2 against the posterior instead of the priors. Pass the posterior samples as `belief`. `evaluate_design` and `optimize_design` then draw $\theta$ from those samples, with a bootstrap resample. The report weights all of the samples, and the search uses their full log-space covariance as $\Sigma_0$. The correlations round 1 left in place are kept, for example a ridge between $k_\text{cat}$ and an inhibition constant. **Efficiency is then relative to the belief:** it is the shrinkage beyond what round 1 already taught, not beyond the priors. A belief has samples but no density to build a proposal from, so the report weights the belief's own samples. Its effective sample size is therefore lower (`ESS min 3` above, see below); longer chains raise it.

Until real data exists, round 1 can be simulated at the model's parameter values, with the same noise model the design was scored under. Continuing the Michaelis–Menten example:

```python
import numpyro.distributions as dist

def measure(design, key):
    """Simulates the design at the model's values and adds the design's noise."""
    config = ctx.SimulationConfig(t1=max(times))
    data = model.simulate(design, config, saveat=design.to_time_matrix())
    for meas in data.measurements:
        key, k = jax.random.split(key)
        y = meas.data["s"]
        meas.data["s"] = y + noise.scale(y) * jax.random.normal(k, y.shape)
    return data

mcmc_config = cmc.MCMCConfig(
    num_warmup=500,
    num_samples=250,
    likelihood=dist.Normal,
    noise_cv=0.1,  # HalfNormal scale of the fitted cv; covers the design's 0.05
    verbose=0,
)

data = measure(result.dataset, jax.random.PRNGKey(2))
fit_1 = cmc.run_mcmc(model, data, 0.5, mcmc_config)  # 0.5: the noise floor

round_2 = cdoe.optimize_design(
    model,
    spec,
    noise,
    n_arms=2,
    times=times,
    key=jax.random.PRNGKey(3),
    n_draws=16,
    belief=fit_1.get_samples(),
)
print(round_2.report)  # shrinkage beyond what round 1 taught

for meas in measure(round_2.dataset, jax.random.PRNGKey(4)).measurements:
    data.add_measurement(meas)
fit_2 = cmc.run_mcmc(model, data, 0.5, mcmc_config)
```

```text
k_m   ±7% (×/÷1.08)  prior ±12%  efficiency 0.40
kcat  ±5% (×/÷1.05)  prior ±9%  efficiency 0.41
maximin 0.40 over 256/256 draws; ESS min 3, median 422
```

The "prior" column is now round 1's posterior. Its 1,000 samples (4 chains of 250) are the weighted samples themselves, so the ESS stays low here (min 3); longer chains raise it.

With lab data, `data` is the measured `Dataset` instead, and nothing else changes. The simulated states that are not observable (`p` and `e` here) can stay in the data: `run_mcmc` fits the observable states only, and warns that the others are unused.

- **Refit all the data under the original priors.** Merge the rounds with `add_measurement` and fit once more. Do not turn the last posterior into the next prior: the posterior is only known through finite samples, and any prior fitted to them is an approximation that compounds over rounds.
- **Match the likelihood to the design's noise model.** `likelihood=dist.Normal` with `noise_cv` set gives the floor-plus-proportional noise of `Proportional`, with the floor's scale from `yerrs`. The default `SoftLaplace` would only rescale the information, but it should still match.
- **Chains run in parallel by default** (`num_chains=4`, `chain_method="parallel"`). `import catalax` sets one CPU device per core, so nothing else is needed. Four chains of the bi-substrate model took 80 s in sequence and 30 s in parallel. To choose the count yourself, call `ctx.set_host_count(n)` right after `import catalax`, before simulating or fitting; the order of imports does not matter. Called later, it raises.
- **`constant=True` parameters** are held at their `value` by `catalax.doe`, but `run_mcmc` still samples them from their priors. Their posterior samples are ignored by `belief`.
- **Simulated data cannot detect a wrong rate law.** The data come from the model being fitted, so the fit is always consistent with it. Checking the mechanism needs real data.
- **Rounds are not free.** In progress-doe, where this module was developed, greedy round-by-round designs lost to one joint design with the same total number of arms. The loop pays off when the prior turns out to be wrong, not by default.

### How far to trust the numbers

The report was checked against NUTS posteriors of the bi-substrate model (enzyme as an unobservable state, `Uniform` priors, `Proportional(0.05, 5)`, 6 time points). The noise was fixed at the truth in NUTS. Ratio of the predicted posterior sd to the NUTS posterior sd, both in log space, for $k_\text{cat}$, $K_{i,u}$, $K_{m,A}$, $K_{m,B}$, with the truth at the geometric midpoints of the priors (mean over 3 noise seeds):

| score | 4 arms | 16 arms (random) |
|---|---|---|
| Laplace (the search) | 0.70 0.71 0.84 0.82 | 0.79 0.80 0.87 0.96 |
| reweighted (the report) | 0.91 0.92 0.96 0.92 | 1.01 1.02 1.03 1.00 |

Over 4 designs × 7 truths drawn from the priors:

| | Laplace | reweighted |
|---|---|---|
| sd ratio to NUTS, 10–90% of cells | 0.81–1.55 | 0.84–1.12 |
| bias in the worst parameter's efficiency | +0.036 | +0.030 |
| rank of designs within a truth (Spearman) | 0.97 | 0.94 |

Laplace errs in both directions: the round Gaussian it puts in place of the box constrains some directions too much and ignores the box's edges in others. With a `LogNormal` prior, which is Gaussian in $\log\theta$, the two scores agree.

**Laplace finds the right design.** A search on the reweighted score landed in the same basin as the Laplace search, and under NUTS the two designs tied: −0.003 ± 0.007 in the worst parameter's efficiency over the 7 truths. Searching on the reweighted score gained nothing.

**Check the effective sample size (ESS).** Each draw's posterior sd is a weighted average over samples, and the ESS counts how many samples effectively carry the weight. The sd's relative Monte Carlo error is about $1/\sqrt{2\,\text{ESS}}$: 7% at 100 and 22% at 10. A small ESS also biases the sd low, so the number becomes optimistic. **With priors** the samples come from a proposal that follows each draw's posterior, so the ESS no longer falls as the design gets more informative. On competitive product inhibition with 2 to 6 arms (1,024 draws), its median stayed at 44,600–50,000 and its minimum at 510–870; weighting prior samples, the median had fallen from 78 to 11 and the minimum to 2. The ±% thus no longer turns optimistic as arms are added. Its worst case, at a box corner or with a near-singular $F$, is about that of weighting 3,277 prior samples. **With a belief** there is no density to follow, so the belief's own samples are weighted, and a design far more informative than round 1 lowers the ESS: with the bi-substrate design's noise divided by 4 (ESS below 10 in every draw), weighting samples gave 0.88–0.92 of a converged sd, and with the noise divided by 16 0.29–0.50. `report.ess_min` and `report.ess_median` show it, and `print(report)` prints both. There is no automatic switch to another estimator: any threshold for it would be arbitrary, and Laplace is not reliable there either. If the ESS is low, read the numbers as an upper bound on how well the parameters will be known, and run longer chains.

What the report does not cover:

- **The likelihood is linearised.** The reweighting uses the exact prior but a Gaussian likelihood built from $F$. On the bi-substrate model that left most predicted sds within about ±15% of NUTS. A model with stronger nonlinearity could do worse.
- **The model and the noise model are assumed correct.** A wrong rate law or a wrong noise level makes every number wrong in ways the design cannot see.
- **The truth is assumed inside the prior box.** The report averages over the priors. If the truth lies outside them, nothing here applies.

## Requirements on priors

`evaluate_design` draws the free parameters from their own `prior`, the same priors `run_mcmc` uses. Every free parameter needs one, and because the design is scored in log parameters the prior must only allow strictly positive values:

- `Uniform(low, high)` with `low > 0`: fine, and the common case
- `LogNormal`, `LogUniform`, and `TruncatedNormal` with `low > 0`: fine
- `Normal`, or `Uniform`/`TruncatedNormal` with `low <= 0`: rejected with an error naming the parameter

The report weights 65,536 samples from these priors. The search needs only the prior variance of $\log\theta$, which is exact for `LogNormal` and estimated from 20,000 prior samples otherwise.

## Solver settings and failed simulations

Information needs accurate derivatives, so both functions default to tight solver tolerances (`rtol = atol = 1e-8`, `max_steps = 4096`). Pass `config=ctx.SimulationConfig(...)` to change them. A simulation that fails never produces NaN in the results: its points contribute nothing to the information, `Information.valid` is `False`, and `evaluate_design` averages only over the prior draws that solved (`DesignReport.n_valid`).
